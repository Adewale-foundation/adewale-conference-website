# A Replacement Can Carry The Outgoing Rep's Exam Identity

Every exam artifact is keyed on the `students` row: the candidate number (`paper_exam_candidates`), the marked sheet (`paper_exam_papers`), the stage result and the attendance mark. Approving a replacement retires the outgoing row and provisions a new one, so a request filed **after** the sitting leaves the number and the score behind on a row the school can no longer see — `get_school_stage_breakdown` filters `deactivated_at`, and the incoming Rep renders blank. `transfer_exam_identity()` re-points those four artifacts, on the reviewing admin's say-so and never by itself.

## Considered options

- **An opt-in transfer at approval time (chosen).** The reviewer ticks a box that names the number and the score being moved, and the RPC moves them in one transaction. The outgoing row stays retired and keeps its own past editions. The decision has to sit with the admin because **the request shape cannot distinguish the two cases**: "the incoming Rep sat on the outgoing Rep's number, correct the name" and "the outgoing Rep sat, then left, and the incoming one never sat" arrive as the same row. Transferring the second hands over a score nobody earned. The cost is a checkbox the reviewer can get wrong, which is why the label prints the number and the mark being moved, and flags `paper_exam_papers.name_mismatch` — a sheet whose bubbled number matched but whose written name did not is direct evidence of the first case.
- **Transfer automatically whenever the outgoing Rep has a paper.** No checkbox, no page reads. Rejected on the mirror case above: it is silent, it is wrong roughly as often as it is right, and the failure is invisible — a school advances on a score its Rep did not sit for, and nothing in the record says so.
- **Rename the `students` row in place instead of retire-and-provision.** The seat is unchanged and only the name moved, so nothing needs re-pointing at all — the cheapest fix by far. Rejected because a `students` row is a person, not a seat (ADR-0001, ADR-0004): renaming it rewrites the outgoing Rep's identity and takes every past year's `student_stage_results` with it, and the access code the outgoing Rep already signs in with would silently become someone else's.
- **Leave it to a manual SQL fix per case.** What happens today. Rejected because the damage is silent and the window is wide: nobody looks until a school asks why its Rep has no score, and by then the cut may have been committed.

## Notes

- **The school's total is not at stake; its account of itself is.** `get_paper_ranking` reads matched papers **without** the `deactivated_at` filter, so the retired Rep's score still reaches the aggregate that drives the cut. What breaks is everything the school and the Rep can see, plus `commit_paper_cut`, which fans the stage outcome down to active Reps only — so the moved result gets no advanced/eliminated decision.
- **The RPC is bounded to a hand-over an approval just made**, not to "move a score from A to B": same school, source retired, target active, and the target holding no record of its own for that exam. `SECURITY DEFINER` granted to `authenticated` is reachable at `/rest/v1/rpc/` with any admin's token (`20260822091000`), so the guard on `registrations` manage is not the only thing standing between it and a rewritten result.
- **A target that already has a record is refused, not merged.** Two sheets for one seat means someone mis-bubbled a number, and picking a winner in SQL would bury that.
- **Scoped to one exam id, never to `edition + stage`.** `unique (edition_year, stage, title)` lets two exams share an edition and a stage, which has already caused one fan-out bug on this table.
- **Not covered:** a replacement filed after the *cut* has been committed. The score moves, but `registration_stage_results` was computed from the aggregate and does not change — which is correct, since the aggregate never counted the retirement.
